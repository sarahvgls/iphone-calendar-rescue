iPhone Calendar Rescue

Lost your calendar? If you have an iPhone backup, your appointments may well still be in it. Drop the file in below and get calendar files you can import anywhere.

Your calendar never leaves this device. There is no upload and no server — the file is opened by your own browser and the conversion happens here, on your computer. You can disconnect from the internet after this page loads and it still works.

Worth a try — but not guaranteed. A backup is a snapshot of one moment. If it was made before your calendar disappeared, your events should be in there. If it was made after, the loss may already be part of it. Use the oldest backup you have, and if one comes up empty, try an older one. Before making a new backup, copy your existing one somewhere safe — a new backup can overwrite the very one that still holds your events.

Getting these onto your iPhone

Email the file to yourself — it really is the best way

  1. Email one of the files to yourself as an attachment. Start with a small calendar rather than the combined one.
  2. Open that email on your iPhone and tap the attachment.
  3. Tap Add All, then pick which calendar to put the events in — make a new one if you can.

AirDrop and Files usually just preview an .ics file instead of offering to import it. Mail hands it to the Calendar app properly.

One file per calendar, so birthdays stay separate from work. Titles, notes, locations, reminders, repeating events and all-day events all come across.

📧 Putting them back on an iPhone? Email the file to yourself.

It sounds primitive, and it is by far the most reliable way. Attach the .ics to an email, open that email on your iPhone, tap the attachment, then tap Add All. AirDrop and the Files app usually just show a preview instead of offering to import.

Questions

Where do I find this file? (step by step)

It is inside the backup your computer already made. There are two ways to get it out.

The quick way — click Choose a whole backup folder above and pick your backup folder. Nothing is uploaded; your browser reads only the calendar out of it. Backups are usually in one of these places, though they are often moved to an external drive:

  • Mac: ~/Library/Application Support/MobileSync/Backup/
  • Windows: C:\Users\you\Apple\MobileSync\Backup
  • Windows (older iTunes): C:\Users\you\AppData\Roaming\Apple Computer\MobileSync\Backup

The careful way — export only the calendar file, so nothing else in the backup is ever touched. This also works on encrypted backups if you know the password. It uses a free program called iTunes Backup Explorer, which runs on Windows, Mac and Linux.

  1. Download it. Open the Releases page and take the newest file for your system: _win_x64.msi on Windows, _mac_arm64.dmg on an M1–M4 Mac, _mac_x64.dmg on an older Intel Mac, or _debian_x64.deb on Linux.
  2. Install it. On Windows, double-click the .msi and follow the installer. On a Mac, open the .dmg and drag the app into Applications. The Windows and Mac downloads include everything they need.
  3. Mac only — give it permission. Skip this on Windows and Linux. Go to System Settings → Privacy & Security → Full Disk Access and switch on iTunes Backup Explorer, then quit and reopen it. macOS hides the backup folder from programs until you do.
  4. Open your backup. The program lists the backups it finds — click one. If it is encrypted, type the backup password.
    If the list is empty, it just did not find them in the standard place. Open the program's settings / preferences and point it at the folder where your backups actually live; they will then appear in the list.
  5. Search for the calendar. Go to the File Search tab. Put HomeDomain in the Domain field, and Library/Calendar/Calendar.sqlitedb% in the Relative Path field — the % at the end matters. Click Search.
  6. Export it. Click Export matching and choose a folder. You may get one to three files — take all of them. On iOS 17.4 and newer your most recent events can live in the -wal file, and exporting only the first one loses them silently.
  7. Bring it back here and drop the Calendar.sqlitedb file into the box at the top of this page.
Can I just upload my whole backup?

You can point this page at your backup folder — that stays on your machine. But nothing is ever uploaded anywhere, by design. A full iPhone backup is 5–100 GB spread over tens of thousands of files, and it contains your messages, photos and health data. Sending all of that to a website to read one small calendar file would be a poor trade, so this tool does not offer it.

My backup is encrypted

Then the calendar inside it is scrambled, and this page cannot read it. You have two options:

  1. Make a fresh backup with “Encrypt local backup” switched off, and use that one.
  2. If you know the backup password, use iTunes Backup Explorer to unlock the backup and export the calendar file, then bring that file back here.

If the password is lost, there is no way around it — not even Apple can reset it.

It says there are no events

Two common reasons.

The backup is too new. If it was made after your calendar disappeared, the empty calendar is what got backed up. Try an older backup if you have one — this is the most common cause, and the reason it is worth keeping old backups rather than overwriting them.

The phone used iCloud Calendar. Then the events live on Apple's servers and were never written into the local backup at all. Sign in at icloud.com and export from there instead.

Is this safe to use?

The page is open source and was written with AI assistance — it has been tested on real backups but not exhaustively reviewed by hand. It only ever reads your file; it cannot change or delete anything in your backup.

You do not have to take the privacy claim on trust. Open your browser's network tab and watch: after the page loads, nothing is sent. Or disconnect from the internet and convert your file anyway — it still works.
